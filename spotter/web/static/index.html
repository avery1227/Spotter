0 · Where is the camera?

The solver will find the height for you — but height and tilt look almost identical from 20 km away, so it can only separate them if some of your points are close to the camera. Include a few things within a kilometre (a piling, the seawall, a roof ridge) or the height it reports will be meaningless.

2 · Put the same spot on the map

In Google Maps, right-click the spot → click the coordinates at the top of the menu to copy them, then paste here.

3 · Describe the line and add

Trace one feature in the frame (click along it), then trace the same feature on the map. The two tracings don't have to start or end at the same place, but the map one must cover everything you traced in the frame; running it longer is fine. Walls, path edges, the waterline and property dividers all work.

Anything not exact gets its height fitted by the solver, within that range. Near the camera a metre matters (about ½° at 100 m), so for the waterline enter the tide height at the time of the frame if you know it.

2 · Freeze, click the plane, pick it

When a plane is in view, press Freeze. The stream is caught on one frame, with every aircraft's ADS-B position at that exact moment. Click where the plane really is in the frame, check it's the right one in the list, and add it. Repeat with other planes or fresh freezes; planes flying in different directions also let the solver measure encoder_delay_s.
